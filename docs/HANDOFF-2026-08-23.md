para dos bloques perfectamente sólidos.

visita, y una cadena que aun así no cierra se **avisa** en vez de desaparecer —
esa parte es la que dejó que esto se publicara. Atravesar el pellizco traza los

## …Y EL PROPIO SMOKE DEL HUD ENCONTRÓ UN ÚLTIMO FALLO

Su salida decía, sobre un **vigorón en hoja**:

> `kios_paseo2 (vigoron) → 40% firme | Se está aguadando la yuca… · ¡La mía sin
> tanto rojo!`

«Sin tanto rojo» es el sirope de cola de un churchill. La barra y el quip
estaban bien —vienen de i18n— pero la frase del cliente no.

**La etiqueta `product` de una frase se autora en `customers.json` y el MUNDO
EMITIDO NO LA LLEVA.** El builder emite id/nombre/posición/distrito/línea, y un
campo fuera de ese modelo se cae sin decir nada: **0 de 24** clientes emitidos
tenían `product`. Con eso `customerLine` tomaba siempre la rama «esta frase
sirve para cualquiera» y **los repuestos por producto no se usaron jamás** —
media función de la tanda anterior, inerte.

Se resuelve **en el cliente**, leyendo la tabla autorada, por las mismas dos
razones que el registro de productos: la etiqueta es una propiedad de la COPIA,
no del lugar donde para el cliente, y afinarla no puede costar 48 minutos. Un
NPC remoto puede traer la suya y esa manda.

**Y la compuerta que lo caza está probada en los dos sentidos**, que es donde
casi se cuela otra vez: el primer barrido que escribí le pasaba a `customerLine`
los registros AUTORADOS —que ya traen `product`— así que **seguía verde con el
arreglo revertido** mientras el HUD decía «Rojito bien fuerte.» sobre un
vigorón. El sujeto tiene que ser `W.CUSTOMERS`, o sea el mundo; la expectativa
viene del archivo. Corregido así, con el arreglo revertido reporta **48 frases
sobre la comida equivocada** y con él puesto, ninguna. Doce frases etiquetadas
por cinco productos, determinista — antes dependía de a quién eligiera el azar.

## A1. The hull answers the helm and nothing else

**Files:** `src/game/boat.js`, `src/game/physics.js`, `src/game/input.js`,
`src/game/ferries.js`, `tools/smoke_boat.mjs`

The premise in the request was that leftover auto-ride code survived. **It did
not.** The only vestige was an unreachable `steeredId` branch in
`advanceFerries` (always called with `null`), now deleted. What actually drove
the boat was live, deliberate code, and all four mechanisms were changed:

| was | now |
|---|---|
| `hullThrottle` idle floor: 22 % throttle under 95 px/s, hands off | **deleted.** One throttle for both media |
| no pivot on water (`physics.js` said "NOTHING ON WATER PIVOTS") | `hullPivot(speed)` — the car's own term, `HULL.pivot 1.5` fading out by `pivotTop 60` |
| `hullBankAssist` pushed 520 px/s² outward unconditionally | it can only **cancel** velocity heading INTO the bank, bounded by the same budget. Stopped or moving away it does nothing |
| `glanceYaw 0.9` rad/s of un-commanded heading on contact | `0.45` |
| no dead-stop clamp for a hull | the clamp is no longer `!afloat` |
| touch `WATER_THR_FLOOR = 0.62` | deleted; both media use `0.35` |

**Measured, and this is the answer to "is the pivot a spinning top": it is not.**

(`+ CHURCHILL`, `+₡250`, `¡SE DERRITIÓ!`), POI tags, POI debug names, the debug
grid, the CERRADO barrier sign, the sponsor plate, the `text` part verb, and the
bridge sign.

The fix is one primitive, `upright(g, x, y, rot)`, which reads the rotation off
the **CTM** (`Math.atan2(m.b, m.a)`) rather than off the `ctx.__worldRot` side
channel the compositor used to stash. That channel only ever knew about the
camera's roll — a sponsor plate already turned by its manzana's `P.ang` stayed
crooked, and nothing could see it. `__worldRot` is deleted.

`ctxRotation(g)` is exported separately and **hoisted out of every loop**:
`getTransform()` mints a DOMMatrix per call and `drawPoiTags` runs per POI.

`shapes.js`'s `text` verb is now upright **by default with an `upright: false`
opt-out**. All four uses in the catalog today are signage (a route shield, the
word BULEVAR, a speed-limit plate); the opt-out exists for the case that verb
also serves and nothing asks for yet — paint on the ground, which must turn with
the road.

**The proof, and it is the whole reason the change is trustworthy:**

- 14 of 15 sheets **byte-identical** (`vehicles actors landmarks parcels scenes
  effects stands kiosks lights feria flora generators sprites`).
- `signs` differs by **52 of 198 000 px** — and `tools/shot-signs.mjs` draws each
  sign twice, the second variant at `ang: 0.45`, precisely because its own header
  says a sign "lives inside a rotation". Those 52 px are the speed-limit numeral
  standing up. That is the fix, photographed.
- `shot-styles` is JSON, not a sheet (it belongs to the theme sweep); compared
  with `tools/json-diff.mjs`: *every computed value IDENTICAL*.
- `pnpm smoke:rotated` gained a mechanism assertion that does not depend on
  pixels at all — it drives `upright()` through three CTMs (camera roll, a

[rot] parcel  -> CTM 0.61 rad,   tras upright() 0 ✓
[rot] both    -> CTM 2.1808 rad, tras upright() 0 ✓
[rot] sin giro -> upright() no hace nada ✓

# D. Perspective — the three.js milestone

The user chose a **real three.js layer** over the cheaper options. It is
tractable, and the reason is a correction worth stating plainly:

> **A tilted ORTHOGRAPHIC camera registers exactly with the 2-D affine one.**
> Under ortho the ground-plane→screen map stays a plain 2-D affine
> (`ScaleY(cosφ) · Rot(θ)`); only the elevation lift falls outside Canvas2D —
> and that is exactly what moves to 3-D.

So ~2 500 lines of ground-plane drawing (`streets.js`, `ground.js`, `water.js`,
`estero.js`, `malecon.js`, `world.js`, `editorWorld.js`) need no rewrite, and
another ~3 400 lines of art painters survive untouched because `shapes.js` and
the `*Shapes.js` family already take their surface as an argument. **Net rewrite
≈ 1 000 lines, not 11 000.**

Two constants that are not free choices:

- **`TILT = acos(0.62) = 51.68°`.** `sunShadow.squashY = 0.62` in `effects.json`
  is the north-south foreshortening the entire 2-D art was authored against, and
  under ortho the foreshortening *is* `cos(tilt)`. Pin `tiltDeg` in
  `src/assets/world-units.json` with a test asserting the two agree. **Do not
  change `squashY`** — that moves art-sheet pixels.
- **The sun's elevation is DERIVED FROM THE EXACT ART OFFSET, not from physical
  solar altitude.** `sunVector()` (`daynight.js:314-321`) is a 2-D fake with a
  hard-coded `y: 0.55`, and the painted north-south component is additionally
  squashed by `sunShadow.squashY = 0.62`. For the authored reach `r`, the Three
  to-sun ray that reproduces the existing pixels is
  `normalize(-sun.x*r, +sun.y*r*0.62, 1)` (`+Y` in Three is north). That gives
  **86.49° at noon and 48.86° at the low-alt clamp**, not the earlier
  79.8°/46.4° estimate, which had incorrectly treated `r` as a unit horizontal
  magnitude and discarded both existing factors. Deriving elevation from
  `asin(sun.alt)` would still throw shadows several times longer than the art.
  Add `sunDirection3()` from this exact displacement and **rewrite
  `sunShadow()` on top of it** so there is one authority and the art sheets
  still diff to zero.

## Things that will bite

- **`#game-canvas` is opaque.** `canvas2d.render` does `clearRect` then
  `drawWaterAll` across the whole viewport, so a three canvas stacked *under* it
  is invisible. The hillshade has to be composited **into** the 2-D frame at the
  right depth (in `c2d/world.js`, before buildings). Measure the
  `drawImage(WebGLCanvas)` cost — it can force a GPU readback on some drivers;
  the fallback is cutting the HUD onto its own canvas at `canvas2d.js:283`
  (everything from that line down is already screen space) and using CSS
  `mix-blend-mode`.
- **Order matters and the obvious order is wrong.** Actors must become billboards
  **before** tilt turns on: once buildings are 3-D and the camera is tilted, a
  pedestrian drawn on the 2-D canvas above cannot be occluded by one. At tilt 0
  nothing occludes anything from directly above, which is what makes the
  layer-by-layer migration safe there.
- **`tools/shot-3d.mjs` is the real deliverable of stage 1.** `CLAUDE.md` says
  never diff a world scene because the noise floor is 2-86 % — that floor is
  `Math.random()` in shake, particles, gulls and ped phases. Freeze the RNG
  before `startExplore`, zero `cam.shake`, and pin the hour **through the live
  module** (the URL-resolution trick `tools/shot-world.mjs:33-38` already uses;
  a plain `import("/src/game/daynight.js")` mints a second `cycle` under HMR and
  that is exactly why `smoke:sky` and `smoke:shadows` were once green for the
  wrong reason). With those three pinned, a world scene **is** diffable.
- **three loads with `await import("three")` + a `manualChunks` entry**, so it
  costs **0 KB while the flag is off** and ~160 KB gzip when on — 1 % of the
  13 MB tile budget. **Never touch `three/examples/jsm`**; that is where the
  number doubles (`CSM` especially — the two-light scheme exists to avoid it).
- Stages 0-5 add **no new world data**. A downsampled global elevation LOD is
  only needed at stage 6, when distant mountains enter the frustum.

## Shadow-map budget

At tilt 0 the camera sees 400 × 225 world px. Worst-case relief is 400 m, and
`reachAtDusk 0.95` caps a shadow at 950 world px, so the caster box is ~2 400 px
a side. At `1024²` that is 0.94 m/texel — against 32 m elevation samples, ample.
Stage 3 uses one terrain `DirectionalLight` and one shadow map. If buildings are
migrated later, they want a different **frustum**, not a bigger map; in Three
r185 light/object `Layers` do not isolate two shadow casters in one render, so a
separate pass is required instead of the earlier proposed two-light/layers
shortcut.

Float32 precision is **not** a problem: 79 400 px has ulp ≈ 0.0095 px and three
differences the matrices in float64 on the CPU. Use per-tile local geometry
anyway because it is free; skip the floating-origin machinery.
